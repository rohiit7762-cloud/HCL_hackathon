0. CRITICAL NOTICES — READ BEFORE PROCEEDING

Never paste credentials into Slack, Zoom chat, or ServiceNow.

0.2 SECURITY SUSPICION: If symptoms indicate a DDoS attack, an unauthorized configuration change, or an intrusion, STOP and invoke Section 9.4 BEFORE rebooting, wiping, or rolling back any device. Evidence preservation takes precedence over speed of restoration.

0.3 EMERGENCY CHANGES: All mitigating changes during a SEV1 are Emergency Changes. Verbal approval by the Incident Commander (IC) is sufficient during the incident. A retroactive ServiceNow Change Request (type: Emergency) must be filed within 24 hours.

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1 This runbook defines the detection, triage, diagnosis, mitigation, escalation, communication, recovery validation, and post-incident review procedures for SEV1 network outages affecting:
    - Primary Data Center DFW-DC1 (Dallas, TX)
    - Secondary / DR Data Center IAD-DC2 (Ashburn, VA)
    - Corporate HQ AUS-HQ (Austin, TX) and regional offices CLT-OFC, DUB-OFC, BLR-OFC
    - Hybrid cloud connectivity (AWS us-east-1, Azure East US 2)
    - Internet edge and customer-facing ingress (portal.corvanta.com, api.corvanta.com)

1.2 Out of scope: application-only outages (APP-RB series) and identity platform outages (IAM-RB-002). Network Engineering supports those incidents in an advisory capacity.

5. DETECTION AND DECLARATION (T+0 to T+15 MINUTES)

5.1 Detection Triggers:
    - PagerDuty alert NET-CORE-DOWN, NET-EDGE-BGP-DOWN, or NET-DX-BOTH-DOWN
    - ThousandEyes: path loss above 50% from 3 or more agents for 3 consecutive rounds
    - Service Desk: 20 or more connectivity tickets within 10 minutes
    - Customer Success: report of portal or API unavailability

5.2 Step 1 — Acknowledge the PagerDuty alert within 5 minutes. Unacknowledged alerts auto-escalate to NetEng-Secondary at 5 minutes and to the Network Engineering Manager at 10 minutes.

5.3 Step 2 — Validate the alert is genuine (not a monitoring failure) from at least two independent vantage points: ThousandEyes agents in two regions AND the jump host.

5.4 Step 3 — Declare SEV1 in Slack:
        /incident declare sev1 "DFW-DC1 network outage - <brief scope>"
    The incident bot creates channel #inc-<YYYYMMDD>-net-sev1, opens the Zoom bridge, creates a ServiceNow P1 incident (assignment group NETENG-L2), and pages the IC.

5.5 Step 4 — Post the Initial Assessment (Appendix B template) within 15 minutes of declaration.

5.6 Step 5 — The IC confirms the role assignments (Technical Lead, Communications Lead, Scribe).

6. TRIAGE AND SCOPING (T+15 to T+30 MINUTES)

6.1 Answer the five scoping questions and post the answers in the incident channel:
    Q1. Which sites, segments, or services are affected?
    Q2. Is impact internal, external (customer-facing), or both?
    Q3. When did impact begin (first alert, UTC)?
    Q4. Was any change implemented on network CIs in the prior 72 hours?
    Q5. Are there security indicators (traffic spikes, unknown sources, threat logs, unexplained config changes)?

6.2 Quick Checks:
    - ThousandEyes: "Corvanta-Edge-Path" view — identify the failing hop.
    - Datadog: "NET | Core Health" — interface errors, CPU, BGP state.
    - ServiceNow change calendar: Change Requests where CI group = "Network-Prod" AND Actual End within the last 72 hours.
    - Splunk:
        index=network (sourcetype=cisco:ios OR sourcetype=arista:eos) (BGP OR OSPF OR "%LINK-3-UPDOWN" OR LINEPROTO) earliest=-2h
        | stats count by host, message | sort - count

    Historical note: 62% of FY2025 SEV1 network incidents were change-induced. Always check Q4 first.

6.3 Diagnostic Decision Tree:
    - Entire DC unreachable, including management    -> 7.1 (OOB access), then 7.3
    - Internet-facing services down, internal OK     -> 7.2 (Edge/ISP) and 7.4 (Firewall)
    - Fabric-wide intermittent loss / high latency   -> 7.5 (L2 loop / storm)
    - Single office or branch down                   -> 7.6 (SD-WAN)
    - Cloud workloads unreachable from on-premises   -> 7.7 (Hybrid cloud)
    - Name resolution failures                       -> 7.8 (DNS/DHCP)
    - Change implemented in prior 72 hours           -> 7.9 (Rollback), in parallel with the above

7. DIAGNOSTIC AND MITIGATION PROCEDURES

7.1 Establish Access
    1. Connect to the jump host via ZPA: ssh <corp-id>@dfw1-jump-01.corp.corvanta.net (Okta + YubiKey).
    2. If the jump host is unreachable, connect through the ZPA OOB segment to dfw1-oob-01 (LTE-backed). Check out the credential from CyberArk Safe "NET-OOB-PROD."
    3. Console port map on dfw1-oob-01: Port 1 = dfw1-edge-rtr01; Port 2 = dfw1-edge-rtr02; Port 3 = dfw1-fw-pa01; Port 4 = dfw1-fw-pa02; Ports 5–8 = dfw1-spine-01 to -04.
    4. If OOB is also unavailable: open a Remote Hands ticket with the colocation provider (target ETA 30 minutes) and escalate immediately to the T+30 tier (Section 9.1).

7.2 Internet Edge / ISP Failure
    1. On dfw1-edge-rtr01 and dfw1-edge-rtr02:
        show bgp ipv4 unicast summary
        show interfaces TenGigabitEthernet0/1/0 | include line protocol|input errors|CRC|rate
        show logging | include BGP-5-ADJCHANGE|LINK-3-UPDOWN
       Expected: ISP neighbor state Established; prefixes received 950,000 or more (full table).
    2. If the ISP-A neighbor is Idle/Active and the interface is down: this is a circuit failure. Open a P1 ticket with ISP-A (circuit LMN-IP-7731045) and confirm traffic has failed over to ISP-B (ThousandEyes path view; "show bgp ipv4 unicast <reference-prefix>" using prefixes in KB0010488).
    3. If ISP-A BGP is Established but blackholing traffic (failover not occurring), shut the ISP-A neighbor with IC approval:
        configure terminal
         router bgp 64500
          neighbor 192.0.2.1 shutdown
        end
       Verify inbound traffic shifts to ISP-B within 3–5 minutes. Log as an Emergency Change.
    4. If BOTH ISPs are down: check router health ("show platform", "show processes cpu sorted | exclude 0.00", power supply status). If not restorable within 30 minutes, prepare for site failover (Section 8).

7.3 Core Routing and Data Center Fabric (Arista EOS)
    1. On dfw1-spine-01 to -04 and affected leaves:
        show ip bgp summary vrf all
        show bgp evpn summary
        show mlag
        show mlag config-sanity
        show interfaces status errdisabled
        show logging last 30 minutes
       Expected: all underlay and EVPN neighbors Estab; MLAG state Active; peer-link Up; no config-sanity inconsistencies.
    2. If a spine shows sustained CPU above 85% or control-plane flapping, drain it with IC approval:
        maintenance
           unit System
              quiesce
       Confirm traffic redistributes across the remaining spines before further troubleshooting.

7.4 Perimeter Firewall (Palo Alto PAN-OS)
    1. On dfw1-fw-pa01 and dfw1-fw-pa02:
        show high-availability state
        show system resources
        show running resource-monitor minute last 5
        show session info
        show counter global filter delta yes severity drop
    2. Thresholds:
       - Dataplane CPU above 90% sustained for 5 minutes, OR session table utilization above 90%: treat as a potential DDoS attack or session exhaustion. Invoke Section 9.4 and engage Marcus Oyelaran (Principal Network Security Engineer) BEFORE applying mitigation.
    3. HA failure (active degraded, passive healthy): on the ACTIVE unit:
        request high-availability state suspend
       Verify the peer transitions to Active. After RCA, restore with:
        request high-availability state functional
       NEVER suspend both units.
    4. Split-brain (both units Active): immediately suspend dfw1-fw-pa02 and open a P1 case with Palo Alto Networks support.

7.5 Layer 2 Loop / Broadcast Storm
    1. Symptoms: fabric-wide packet loss, leaf CPU spikes, MAC flap log entries.
        show interfaces counters rates | nz
        show logging | include MACFLAP|MLAG|STP
        show spanning-tree blockedports
    2. Identify the interface with abnormal broadcast/multicast packets-per-second and shut it with IC approval:
        configure
         interface Ethernet<x>
          shutdown
    3. Post-incident: confirm storm-control is configured on all host-facing ports ("storm-control broadcast level 1").

7.6 Branch / SD-WAN
    1. In SD-WAN Manager: Monitor > Devices — verify edge reachability and control connections.
    2. On the affected edge:
        show sdwan control connections
        show sdwan bfd sessions
    3. Edges fail over to LTE automatically. If LTE failover does not occur, engage the carrier and dispatch local IT.
    4. A branch outage is SEV1 ONLY if AUS-HQ is fully down (25% or more of the workforce). Otherwise, downgrade to SEV2.

7.7 Hybrid Cloud Connectivity
    AWS:
        aws directconnect describe-virtual-interfaces --region us-east-1 --query "virtualInterfaces[].{id:virtualInterfaceId,state:virtualInterfaceState,bgp:bgpPeers[0].bgpStatus}"
        aws ec2 describe-transit-gateway-attachments --filters Name=transit-gateway-id,Values=tgw-0c4e1a9b27f3d5e81
        aws ec2 describe-vpn-connections --vpn-connection-ids vpn-0b7d3e19c4a2f6d08 --query "VpnConnections[].VgwTelemetry"
      On dfw1-dx-rtr01: show bgp ipv4 unicast neighbors 169.254.10.1 | include BGP state
      If both DX circuits are down, confirm the backup VPN tunnels are UP and carrying routes. Engage Elena Vasquez-Hart (Principal Cloud Architect) and open an AWS Enterprise Support case at severity "Business-critical system down."
    Azure:
        az network express-route show --name er-corvanta-eus2-01 --resource-group rg-network-prod --query "{circuit:circuitProvisioningState, provider:serviceProviderProvisioningState}"
        az network express-route list-route-tables --name er-corvanta-eus2-01 --resource-group rg-network-prod --path primary --peering-name AzurePrivatePeering
      Engage Rohan Mehta (Senior Cloud Architect — Azure & Hybrid) and open a Microsoft Unified Support Severity A case.

7.8 DNS / DHCP
        dig @10.10.5.10 portal.corvanta.com +short
        dig @10.20.5.10 portal.corvanta.com +short
    If DFW resolvers fail but IAD resolvers respond, clients will use the secondary resolver (10.20.5.10) via DHCP option 6. Check Infoblox Grid Manager > Grid > Members for service status and restart the DNS service on the affected member with IC approval.

7.9 Rollback of a Recent Change
    1. Compare the running configuration against the last known-good version in Oxidized (git repository "netconfig-backups").
    2. Rollback commands (IC approval required):
       - Cisco IOS-XE:  show archive
                        configure replace bootflash:<archive-file> list
       - Arista EOS:    configure replace checkpoint:<checkpoint-name>
       - Palo Alto:     configure
                        load config version <version-number>
                        commit
    3. If the offending change has NO approved ServiceNow Change Request, or was made by an unrecognized account: treat it as a potential security incident. Invoke Section 9.4 and DO NOT roll back until the SOC has captured the running configuration as evidence.

8. SITE FAILOVER / DISASTER RECOVERY

8.1 Trigger: DFW-DC1 is not recoverable within 60 minutes of declaration, OR a facility event occurs (power, cooling, fire, or physical access loss).

8.2 Authority: The IC recommends; Victor Ahn (VP, Infrastructure) or Linda Marchetti (CIO) approves.

8.3 Procedure summary (the full procedure is in DR-PLAN-003):
    1. Validate IAD-DC2 health using Sections 7.2–7.4 against iad2-* devices.
    2. Disable DFW members in GSLB wide-IP pools for portal.corvanta.com and api.corvanta.com:
        tmsh modify gtm pool a <pool-name> members modify { <dfw-member> { disabled } }
    3. Apply pre-staged route-map RM-DR-ADVERTISE on iad2-edge-rtr01/02 to advertise 198.51.100.0/24 from IAD-DC2.
    4. The DBA team executes database failover per DB-RB-004.
    5. Validate synthetic transactions for portal and API (100% success for 15 minutes).
    Recovery objectives (Tier 1 services): RTO 4 hours; RPO 15 minutes.

11. RECOVERY VALIDATION — EXIT CRITERIA

All of the following must be true before the IC declares "Resolved":
    [ ] All BGP sessions Established on edge, fabric, and DX routers at both data centers
    [ ] ThousandEyes: packet loss below 1% and latency within 20% of baseline for 30 consecutive minutes
    [ ] Firewall HA pairs in Active/Passive with configuration synchronized
    [ ] Portal and API synthetic transactions at 100% success for 15 minutes
    [ ] No new related alerts for 30 minutes
    [ ] Service Desk ticket inflow returned to baseline
    [ ] ServiceNow incident updated with resolution code and timeline
    [ ] Temporary mitigations (e.g., shut BGP neighbors, drained spines) tracked as follow-up Change Requests to restore redundancy within 5 business days

12. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW (PIR)

12.1 A blameless PIR meeting is held within 5 business days of resolution.
12.2 The RCA document (5 Whys, Scribe timeline, contributing factors) is published to Confluence space NETOPS within 10 business days.
12.3 Corrective actions are logged in Jira project NETOPS with label "sev1-capa." P1 actions are due within 30 days.
12.4 Metrics recorded: MTTA, MTTR, customer-minutes impacted, SLA impact.
12.5 This runbook is updated whenever the PIR identifies a procedural gap.
